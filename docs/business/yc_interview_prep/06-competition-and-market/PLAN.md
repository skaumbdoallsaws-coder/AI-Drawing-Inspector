# Area 06: Competition and Market

## Schedule

- Start: August 9, 2026
- End: August 10, 2026
- Time: 2 days, 4 hours

## What This Area Must Address

This area establishes specific knowledge of alternatives and a credible venture-scale market. It must avoid vague statements that competitors manage files while InspectorPro understands them unless concrete product differences support the claim.

The work must address:

- Manual work as the current primary alternative.
- PLM, PDM, QMS, engineering-AI, and generic-AI competitors.
- What each alternative does well and where it stops.
- Why customers would choose InspectorPro.
- Why incumbents cannot immediately neutralize the wedge.
- A bottom-up estimate of reachable customers and contract value.
- The first segment and adjacent expansion markets.
- What could make the market smaller than expected.

## Core Questions

- Who are the closest competitors?
- How do customers solve this today?
- What is specifically wrong with those alternatives?
- Why will a CAD or PLM vendor not copy you?
- What do you understand that competitors do not?
- How many first-segment customers exist?
- How large can this become from the bottom up?
- Which adjacent markets follow the initial wedge?

## Daily Plan

### August 9: Competition

- Build a comparison based on workflows, users, data, and actions.
- Identify the strongest competitor rather than an easy comparison.
- Prepare honest answers about incumbent response and model commoditization.

### August 10: Market

- Calculate a bottom-up market from target companies, likely adoption, and annual contract value.
- Define the first reachable segment separately from the long-term market.
- Explain expansion without relying on an arbitrary market-percentage claim.

## Deliverables

- Competitor and alternative matrix.
- Strongest-competitor response.
- Incumbent-copying analysis.
- Bottom-up market worksheet.
- Initial segment definition.
- Adjacent-market sequence.
- Eight to ten direct-answer cards.

## Definition of Done

This area is complete when Shadrack can name the strongest alternatives, explain specific product differences without dismissing competitors, and derive a large market from customers and contract values rather than top-down percentages.
