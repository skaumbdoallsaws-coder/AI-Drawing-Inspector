# Area 01: Problem

## Schedule

- Start: July 20, 2026
- End: July 23, 2026
- Time: 4 days, 8 hours

## What This Area Must Address

This area establishes the problem InspectorPro solves before discussing product features. It must make the manufacturing workflow understandable to someone who has never used CAD or participated in a supplier-quality decision.

The work must address:

- The exact product-information problem.
- The first person who experiences it.
- The manual workflow used today.
- How frequently the problem occurs.
- The cost of delay, error, and coordination.
- Why existing systems leave the context scattered.
- How Shadrack encountered the problem personally.
- Evidence that the problem exists beyond one employer or piston example.
- Why the problem is becoming more important now.
- Which claims are observed facts and which remain hypotheses.

## Core Questions

- What exactly is the problem?
- Who has this problem most acutely?
- What happens today when a supplier dimension fails inspection?
- How painful and frequent is the problem?
- What does ignoring it cost?
- Why has PLM, PDM, CAD, and quality software not solved it?
- How did you discover the problem?
- What evidence convinced you it is widespread?
- Why is now the right time to solve it?
- What part of the problem remains unvalidated?

## Daily Plan

### July 20: Define the Core Problem

- Describe the current workflow without mentioning InspectorPro features.
- Identify the first user and the job they are trying to complete.
- Separate the underlying problem from its symptoms.
- Produce a one-sentence and three-sentence problem explanation.
- Record an unprepared baseline answer before revising it.

### July 21: Pain, Frequency, and Cost

- List the delays, handoffs, mistakes, and duplicated work caused by scattered context.
- Identify which costs are measured, remembered from experience, or estimated.
- Build one defensible example of time or money lost.
- Remove dramatic claims that cannot be supported.

### July 22: Discovery, Evidence, and Why Now

- Document the strongest firsthand story from Shadrack's manufacturing work.
- Summarize customer or practitioner feedback received so far.
- Explain why the problem has persisted.
- Explain what changes in AI, CAD extraction, and manufacturing make the product possible now.

### July 23: Problem Fluency

- Answer the core questions in random order.
- Explain the problem once to a manufacturing expert and once to a general audience.
- Challenge the assumptions: Is this urgent, frequent, and expensive enough?
- Revise only answers that are indirect, vague, or unsupported.

## Deliverables

- One-sentence problem definition.
- Three-sentence problem explanation.
- Current-workflow diagram or written sequence.
- First-user definition.
- Evidence table separating facts, estimates, and unknowns.
- One concrete founder story.
- Five to ten direct-answer cards.
- List of evidence gaps to address through customer conversations.

## Definition of Done

This area is complete when Shadrack can explain the problem in under 20 seconds without product jargon, support it with a real example, quantify only what is defensible, and answer why the problem exists and why now without drifting into a product pitch.
