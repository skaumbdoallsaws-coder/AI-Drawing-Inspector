# InspectorPro YC Interview Preparation

## Purpose

This workspace organizes a 30-day preparation cycle for a possible Y Combinator interview. The cycle starts July 20, 2026 and ends August 18, 2026. The schedule reserves two hours per day, for a total of 60 hours.

The objective is founder fluency, not memorized scripts. By the end of the cycle, Shadrack should be able to answer direct, factual questions about InspectorPro naturally, support answers with evidence, demonstrate the product quickly, and discuss uncertainty honestly.

## Operating Principles

1. Answer the exact question in the first sentence.
2. Use plain language before manufacturing or CAD terminology.
3. State observed facts separately from estimates and future plans.
4. Support important claims with one number, example, or customer observation.
5. Stop after answering and let the interviewer choose the follow-up.
6. Do not force prepared talking points into unrelated questions.
7. Do not inflate users, pilots, savings, accuracy, or commitments.
8. Treat product and customer progress as more valuable than rehearsal.
9. Keep the working demo ready, but do not prepare a formal presentation.
10. Update answers whenever new evidence changes the underlying facts.

## Three Answer Depths

Each important question should have three available levels:

- Direct answer: 5 to 10 seconds.
- Supporting evidence: 10 to 20 additional seconds.
- Deeper explanation: only when the interviewer requests it.

The direct answer is not a slogan. It must answer the question. The evidence layer should add one concrete fact or example. The deeper layer should demonstrate command of the subject without becoming a speech.

## Standard Two-Hour Session

- 15 minutes: update product progress, metrics, customer conversations, and facts.
- 40 minutes: study the day's subject and resolve gaps in understanding.
- 25 minutes: write or revise direct answers and supporting evidence.
- 20 minutes: answer randomized questions aloud.
- 10 minutes: review the recording for clarity, length, and unsupported claims.
- 10 minutes: record evidence gaps and actions that would improve the answers.

## Required Final Artifacts

By August 18, this workspace should contain:

- A one-page company fact sheet with current metrics and dates.
- A concise knowledge map for every interview area.
- Direct and evidence-level answers for the highest-probability questions.
- A customer and pilot status ledger that distinguishes interest from commitment.
- A bottom-up market and pricing model with stated assumptions.
- A competitor comparison based on specific product limitations.
- A technical trust sheet covering accuracy, security, model use, and failure handling.
- An application claim ledger reconciling the written application and both videos.
- A live-demo checklist and a tested backup path.
- An adversarial objection bank with candid answers.
- A dated progress log showing what changed after the application was submitted.

## Progress Ledger

Update these facts at the start of each session:

- Product capabilities working today.
- Product capabilities that still require founder assistance.
- Number of customer conversations.
- Number of live product demonstrations.
- Number and status of pilot discussions.
- Signed pilots, letters of intent, users, revenue, and usage, if any.
- Measured workflow time, model cost, latency, and accuracy, where available.
- Most important product improvement completed since applying.
- Most important customer learning since applying.
- Largest unresolved risk.

## After the 30-Day Cycle

Until an interview decision arrives, maintain readiness with two short sessions per week:

- One session to update facts, customers, metrics, and product progress.
- One session to answer randomized questions and verify the demo path.

Do not continue adding scripts. Replace outdated answers with current evidence.
