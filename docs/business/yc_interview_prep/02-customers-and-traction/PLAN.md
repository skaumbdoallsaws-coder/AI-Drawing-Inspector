# Area 02: Customers and Traction

## Schedule

- Start: July 24, 2026
- End: July 28, 2026
- Time: 5 days, 10 hours

## What This Area Must Address

This area identifies the first market narrowly and establishes what real-world evidence exists. It must distinguish a user, beneficiary, buyer, decision-maker, and pilot sponsor. It must also distinguish a conversation, demonstration, expression of interest, pilot discussion, signed commitment, active user, and paying customer.

The work must address:

- The ideal first customer profile.
- The first user and their daily workflow.
- The economic buyer and approval chain.
- How customers solve the problem today.
- What customers have actually said or done.
- Exact pilot status and remaining conditions.
- Adoption barriers and security concerns.
- What would cause usage, retention, and expansion.
- The metrics that will demonstrate demand.

## Core Questions

- Who is the first customer?
- Who uses InspectorPro and who pays?
- Who approves a pilot?
- How many people have seen or used the product?
- What did they ask for next?
- What is the exact status of each pilot discussion?
- Why would a customer buy now?
- What prevents a customer from trying it?
- What behavior would demonstrate retention?
- What customer evidence would change your strategy?

## Daily Plan

### July 24: First Customer and User

- Define company size, industry, CAD environment, and workflow maturity.
- Identify the first individual user and their urgent job.
- Explain why this segment is the fastest credible entry point.

### July 25: Buyer and Adoption Path

- Identify users, champions, buyers, IT/security reviewers, and executives.
- Map how a pilot decision is made.
- Describe the current alternative and switching cost.

### July 26: Customer Evidence

- Inventory every relevant conversation, demo, and feedback session.
- Record dates, roles, exact observations, and requested follow-ups.
- Separate direct evidence from interpretation.

### July 27: Traction and Pilot Truth

- Define the current stage of each possible pilot.
- Record what is agreed, what is being discussed, and what is not committed.
- Define pilot entry criteria, success metrics, and conversion criteria.

### July 28: Objections, Retention, and Fluency

- Prepare candid answers about security, integration, accuracy, budget, and workflow change.
- Explain why users would return and recommend the product.
- Run randomized customer and traction questions.

## Deliverables

- Ideal-customer profile.
- User, buyer, champion, and decision-maker map.
- Customer-conversation ledger.
- Pilot-status ledger with exact language.
- Current-alternative workflow.
- Adoption-objection list.
- Initial usage and retention metrics.
- Ten to fifteen direct-answer cards.

## Definition of Done

This area is complete when every traction claim can be verified, the first customer is specific, and Shadrack can clearly explain who uses, buys, approves, and benefits from InspectorPro without treating interest as commitment.
